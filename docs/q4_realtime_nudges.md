# Q4: Live insights and nudges from call audio

Code: [`backend/app/realtime`](../backend/app/realtime) (ASR, signals, nudges, session),
[`backend/app/api/realtime.py`](../backend/app/api/realtime.py) (WebSocket),
[`frontend/src/features/nudges`](../frontend/src/features/nudges) (dashboard), and
[`configs/nudges/rules.json`](../configs/nudges/rules.json) (all thresholds and rules). Measured results are in
[`evidence/q4/latency_report.md`](../evidence/q4/latency_report.md); per-event logs are in `evidence/q4/event_logs/`.

## 1. Streaming method

- **Input.** Either a dual-channel call recording (channel 0 = agent, 1 = customer) replayed at 1x real time, or a live
  microphone. Replay reads 100 ms of PCM16 at 16 kHz, waits until that audio would have been spoken, and only then
  hands it to ASR. Nothing is processed ahead of real time, and the dashboard plays the same audio in sync, so nudges
  visibly appear while the call is still playing.
- **Live modes.** `mic` streams the browser microphone, downsampled to 16 kHz PCM, over the same WebSocket.
  `browser_asr` sends Web Speech API transcripts instead of audio.
- **Test calls.** Five calls are generated by `backend/scripts/generate_call_audio.py` from scripted dialogues
  (Windows SAPI voices, one per speaker, with background noise mixed in at the listed SNR). Each has a timeline with
  the exact reference text, used for labelling and WER.
- **ASR.** One streaming recognizer per channel, run in a worker thread so the event loop stays free.
  - Default: Vosk small en-US (offline, CPU).
  - With `ASR_PROVIDER=deepgram`: Deepgram Nova-3 with `multichannel=true`.
  - Partial and final hypotheses, word timings, and word confidences flow into the signal engine.
  - Channel separation gives exact agent/customer labels, so no diarization is needed.

## 2. Signal design

Signals are deterministic first (rules, lexicons, call-state checks) and use an LLM only where judgment is needed.

| Signal | Detection | Speaker | Example nudge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compliance: risky claim | Regex: *guaranteed*, *one hundred percent*, *definitely get*, *no risk at all* | Agent | "Risky claim: approval cannot be guaranteed. Correct it now..." |
| Compliance: fee evasion | Agent deflects a fee question (*it is very small*, *don't worry about the fees*) | Agent | "State the processing fee (up to 2% plus GST) and point to the Key Fact Statement." |
| Compliance: recording disclosure missing | **State check.** No disclosure phrase from the agent within the first 20 s. Matching is fuzzy (*recorded*, *recording*, *monitored*, or "call is \<any\>-ed for quality", which tolerates ASR hearing *reported*), and the check runs only when agent-channel ASR confidence is at least 0.88, so noise can't create a false "missing" | Agent | "Recording disclosure missing. Tell the customer the call is recorded." |
| Compliance: pricing before disclosure | **State check.** A rate is quoted before any "subject to credit assessment" disclosure | Agent | "Add: final terms are subject to credit assessment." |
| Missed cross-sell | *second car*, *another vehicle*, *son just got his driving licence*, *expanding to a new shop*. If the agent never acts on the nudge, it is marked `missed_opportunity` at call end | Customer | "Offer the multi-vehicle discount on a second policy." |
| Rising frustration | Weighted lexicon (*ridiculous*, *how many times*, *are you even listening*) accumulated with exponential decay (half-life 40 s); fires at 0.9, so one mild word doesn't trigger it | Customer | "Pause the script, acknowledge the concern, summarize what you will do next." |
| Payment difficulty | *lost my job*, *cannot pay*, *salary delayed*, *medical emergency* | Customer | "Offer the approved payment-support or restructuring callback; do not push for payment now." |
| Callback need | *call me later*, *I am driving*, *bad line*, *signal is weak* | Customer | "Offer to schedule a callback at a better time." |
| Buying signal | *how do I apply*, *I wanted to renew*, *can I pay online* | Customer | "Confirm the next step and close." |
| Topic shifts | Keyword topic map per utterance; shifts are emitted as `topic` events and shown on the dashboard timeline | Both | none (context only) |

When `OPENAI_API_KEY` is set, final customer utterances that no rule matched, and that have at least 5 words, go to
GPT-4o-mini with a JSON schema. The output is limited to the same signal kinds with confidence and evidence, then passes
through the same gates. It's off in the measured runs, so the LLM latency row is reported as n/a rather than estimated.

**Early triggering on partials.** Every pattern-based signal can fire from partial hypotheses, but only when the same
match appears in two consecutive partials, because partial text gets revised. Vosk partials report a fixed confidence
of 1.0, so partial confidence isn't used as a gate. When the final transcript arrives, it merges into the existing card
as a duplicate instead of creating a second nudge.

## 3. Nudge control

`NudgeManager.offer()` applies the gates in order. Every rejection is logged with its reason, and the dashboard lists
suppressed candidates live.

1. **Confidence:** signal confidence of at least 0.6 (per-kind overrides allowed). Confidence is the rule weight scaled
   by ASR confidence.
2. **ASR quality:** mean word confidence of at least 0.55 (final transcripts).
3. **Minimum length:** at least 3 words (final transcripts), so "hmm okay" can't trigger anything.
4. **Duplicate:** the same kind and topic as an active card is merged into that card's evidence (no new alert).
5. **Already delivered:** the same kind and topic delivered earlier in the call is not repeated after it closes.
6. **Cooldown with topic grouping:** within the kind's cooldown (25 s default, 10 s for compliance), a new topic of the
   same kind is folded into the visible card ("Also: ...") instead of becoming a new one.
7. **Max active (3) with priority eviction:** a priority-1 compliance nudge evicts the oldest lowest-priority card. A
   lower-priority nudge never evicts a higher one.
8. **Expiry:** each kind has a TTL (30 to 60 s). An unacted card closes as `expired_unactioned`.
9. **Resolution:** when the agent says the recommended thing (for example, mentions the multi-vehicle discount), the
   card closes as `resolved_by_agent`. Cross-sell cards still open at call end become `missed_opportunity`.

## 4. Latency instrumentation

Every nudge carries timestamps for each stage:

- **Reference:** the wall time the audio containing the end of the trigger word was due to arrive. This uses ASR word
  timings, and uses the real-time schedule rather than when ASR got to it, so backlog counts as latency.
- **ASR result, signal, nudge decision, server send.**
- **Display:** the dashboard and the evaluation client acknowledge each nudge with `Date.now()` at render time.

End-to-end = display minus reference. Component rows in the report:

- ASR finalization lag
- ASR compute per 100 ms chunk per channel (the "transcription latency per chunk" the brief asks for)
- Signal extraction
- Nudge control
- Delivery

`python backend/scripts/run_realtime_eval.py --rounds 3` streams all five calls concurrently, three times, through the
real WebSocket and server. It then runs a one-call-at-a-time baseline.

## 5. Results

From [`evidence/q4/latency_report.md`](../evidence/q4/latency_report.md), on a 12-thread laptop CPU with Vosk and rules
only:

| Stage | P50 (ms) | P95 (ms) |
|---|---|---|
| End-to-end, trigger word to nudge displayed (5 concurrent calls) | 2,079 | 3,221 |
| End-to-end, single call | 2,157 | 3,085 |
| ASR finalization lag | 920 | 1,134 |
| ASR compute per 100 ms chunk per channel | 26 | 123 |
| Signal extraction (rules) | 0.2 | 0.5 |
| Nudge control | 0.05 | 0.5 |
| Delivery, server to browser | 0.7 | 1.6 |

**Where the time goes.** Rules, nudge control and delivery together take under 5 ms. Almost all of the roughly 2 s is
the ASR model: measured directly, Vosk small puts a word into its partial hypothesis 1.1 to 2.1 s after the word ends.
Concurrency barely changes the numbers, so this is model latency, not CPU contention. Nudges still arrive well before
they stop being useful. Lead time before call end was 13 to 36 s for every nudge except one: the fee-evasion nudge came
0.8 s before the end, because the scripted deflection happens in the last seconds of that call. Swapping in a
low-latency streaming ASR (the Deepgram adapter) is the obvious next lever. It hasn't been measured here because it
needs an API key.

**How the measurement improved during the build.** Earlier versions of this report showed about 0.8 s end-to-end,
because the reference was the end of the whole utterance. That hid the time spent waiting for the speaker to finish the
sentence. Measuring from the trigger word itself first gave 2.7 s P50 and 6.5 s P95 (finals only). Enabling partial
triggers for customer-side signals brought that to the numbers above.

## 6. False-positive analysis

Labels: each scenario lists `required` nudge kinds and `allowed` kinds. Any emitted kind outside both lists counts as a
false positive.

| Scenario | Required | Emitted (each of 3 rounds) | False positives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rt_cross_sell` (SNR 28 dB) | cross_sell | cross_sell (+ add-driver topic grouped into it), buying_signal | 0 |
| `rt_compliance` | compliance | risky_claim (with recording disclosure and pricing grouped in), fee_evasion | 0 |
| `rt_frustration` | frustration, payment_difficulty | both | 0 |
| `rt_noisy` (SNR 4 dB) | none | none | 0 |
| `rt_holdout_noisy` (SNR 6 dB, held out) | none | none | 0 |

18 nudges were emitted across 15 runs, with 0 false positives and every required kind detected. 46 repeat or low-value
candidates were suppressed: 37 duplicates and 9 cooldown groupings. Without the controls, the frustration call alone
would have raised 5 separate frustration alerts in 25 seconds; it raised one card whose evidence kept growing.

Read these numbers with care:

- **Tuned on the same calls.** Two false positives were found and fixed during development:
  - The pricing-before-disclosure rule fired on "premium is" in the insurance call; the pattern was narrowed.
  - In the 4 dB call, ASR heard "recorded" as "reported", so the missing-disclosure check fired wrongly. The phrase
    match was made fuzzy, and the check now requires agent-channel ASR confidence of at least 0.88.
  - Because of that tuning, `rt_holdout_noisy` was written afterwards as an unseen negative control. It passed. It is
    one call, so it is a sanity check, not a measured false-positive rate. Partial triggering was enabled after the
    holdout existed and was evaluated on all five calls, including the holdout.
- **Noise costs recall as well as precision.** In `rt_noisy`, the customer said "the line is very bad" and "the signal
  is weak". ASR produced "the line is very that" and "the seeking this week", so a callback nudge that would have
  helped never fired. ASR word error rate on that customer channel is 47% (see
  [`evidence/q4/asr_wer.md`](../evidence/q4/asr_wer.md)), against 4 to 11% on the clean calls. The gates prevented
  noise from creating alerts, but they can't recover words the ASR never produced.
- **Small, synthetic sample.** Five scripted calls with TTS voices. Real precision needs a few hundred labelled real
  calls, with reviewers marking each nudge as useful, unnecessary or wrong.

## 7. Limitations at 10x scale and with noisy audio

See [`production_improvements.md`](production_improvements.md), sections 5 and 6. In short:

- At 10x, ASR is the cost and latency driver. Move to managed or GPU streaming ASR, shard calls across workers, keep
  the signal engine co-located with ASR, and fan nudges out through a pub/sub gateway.
- Rules cost under 1 ms per utterance and scale linearly. An LLM classifier would need a per-call budget and a
  rules-only fallback when it is slow.
- With noisy audio, expect fewer nudges rather than wrong ones under the current gates. Improving recall needs better
  ASR (narrowband-tuned models, noise suppression) and thresholds tuned per market on real labelled calls.
